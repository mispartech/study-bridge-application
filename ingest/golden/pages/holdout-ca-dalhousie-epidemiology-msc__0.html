Epidemiology and Applied Health Research (MSc)

Build strong health system research skills you can use in real‑world settings.

Why choose this program?

A master's degree in Epidemiology and Applied Health Research offers opportunities to gain an understanding of how health and disease affect populations while building practical research skills with a thesis project that aligns with your goals.

Pursue your graduate studies in epidemiology and applied health research at Dalhousie and learn how to design studies, analyze data, and interpret evidence that informs health decisions. You'll work closely with faculty who support your academic and research interests. 

Your thesis project provides in‑depth research experience. The optional practicum placement allows an opportunity for applied work alongside coursework. Both help you develop strong skills in epidemiology and applied health research. 

The program is delivered in person in Halifax and is designed for students who want focused, graduate‑level training in epidemiology and research methods. 

Careers

Graduates use their training to work in health data analysis, generate and appraise research evidence, and address epidemiological, population health, and applied health system issues across many settings. 

This degree offers solid foundational skills in epidemiology and health research you can apply in public health, research, and health systems settings. Your career options include membership in research teams, health systems or government settings where you may support research studies, conduct data analysis, or help inform health policy and programs. Career paths vary based on your experience and interests.

Possible careers include:

  • Epidemiologist  
  • Health research analyst  
  • Public health analyst  
  • Research coordinator  
  • Policy or program analyst 

Program options

The program offers flexible options for coursework and thesis topics to support different research and career goals. 

Standard program duration:

  • Full time: 2 years
  • Part-time: 4 years

Enrolment options:

  • Full-time
  • Part-time

Delivery format:

In-person

Course options

  • Core courses in epidemiology and biostatistics. 
  • Elective course options in secondary data analysis, advanced statistical methods, infectious diseases, global health, clinical epidemiology, and evidence synthesis. 

All graduate programs at Dalhousie are collaboratively delivered by a home Faculty and the Faculty of Graduate Studies.

Admission requirements

You'll need to meet the Faculty of Graduate Studies minimum requirements as well as any program-specific admission requirements before you can apply.

Learn more about the admissions requirements for international students.

The next deadline to apply for this program is November 1 of the year prior to anticipated admission for those who wish to be considered for Dalhousie University scholarships and other external funding opportunities. 

January 31 is the deadline for all other completed applications.

 

Program-specific requirements

This program requires you to meet the following criteria:

  • An honours undergraduate degree or equivalent.
  • An academic background appropriate for epidemiology or applied health research.

Proof of English language competency if the first language is not English. (TOEFL iBT 100; IELTS 7.5; EAP2 A) 

For more information about other English language proficiency tests, please visit the  Faculty of Graduate Studies English language proficiency requirements.

While you are not required to identify a supervisor prior to applying to the program, applicants are encouraged to review faculty members’ research interests for alignment with their own. 

Explore our faculty directory.

As part of your application, you should plan to include:

  • Official transcripts from all post-secondary institutions
  • Reference letter
  • A one-page "personal statement" that includes
    • Your professional interests and career objectivs
    • Your research interests related to the MSc program in Epidemiology and Applied Health Research
  • A one-page "quantitative training and experience" document that includes
    • Description of courses in statistics or other quantitative courses
    • Any practical experience with quantitative analyses
    • If applicable, list any relevant statistics courses that are shown on your university transcripts, when you took each course (year and term) and the general topics that were covered. 
    • If applicable, provide a concise description of the types of statistical analyses that you have conducted in the past two years.
  • A curriculum vitae providing an overview of your qualifications and experience, including
    • Academic history with all post-secondary institutions and degrees
    • Progessional, volunteer, general leadership experience and history
    • Publications and presentations
    • Awards and honours
